Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC)
A strategic approach to coordinating all marketing communications to ensure consistency of message and complementary use of promotional tools across all channels.
Communication Process
The path a message travels from Source → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver, with noise potentially disrupting any stage and feedback completing the loop.
Encoding
The first step in communication — converting ideas into words, pictures, symbols, or other elements that can be transmitted to a target audience.
Decoding
The process by which the receiver interprets and makes sense of the message sent by the source.
Noise
Any personal or environmental interference — competing ads, distractions, cultural misinterpretation — that prevents a marketing message from being received or understood correctly.
Feedback
The receiver's response to a message, which flows back to the source. In digital marketing, this includes click-through rates, purchases, and social media engagement.
Retargeting
A type of online advertising that targets consumers based on their previous internet activities (e.g., showing ads for products a user previously viewed).
Promotional Tools
The six elements of the promotional mix (advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, direct marketing, public relations, digital marketing) used together for greater impact.
Promotion
All marketing communications that inform and persuade consumers to purchase products and services, including advertising, digital marketing, PR, direct marketing, personal selling, and sales promotion.
Promotional Mix
The six marketing communication tools: advertising, sales promotion, public relations, direct marketing, personal selling, and digital marketing.
Promotional Objectives
The goals that guide a brand's communication choices — to inform, remind, engage, or entertain consumers based on their stage in the buying process.
Primary Demand
Promotion designed to create demand for a product category as a whole (e.g., "Got Milk?" promotes all milk, not a specific brand).
Selective Demand
Promotion aimed at building preference for a specific brand within a product category (e.g., a Lucerne dairy ad promoting its milk over competitors).
AIDA Model
A framework showing how consumers move from Awareness → Interest → Desire → Action when exposed to promotional messages.
Information Processing Model
Views the consumer as a problem solver moving through stages: Exposure → Attention → Comprehension → Yielding → Retention → Behavior. Retention is a distinguishing feature of this model.
Sales Promotion
A promotional tool using incentives (coupons, contests, loyalty programs, flash sales) designed to create an immediate and measurable consumer response.
Trade-Oriented Sales Promotion
Sales promotions aimed at wholesalers and retailers (e.g., slotting fees, promotional allowances) to move products through the distribution channel.
Consumer-Oriented Sales Promotion
Sales promotions directed at end consumers (e.g., coupons, BOGO offers, sweepstakes) to drive immediate purchase behavior.
Public Relations (PR)
A promotional tool that aims to improve an organization's image or sway public attitudes through earned media, press releases, events, and community engagement.
Direct Marketing
A promotional method that communicates directly with target customers through mail, email, telemarketing, or digital channels to generate a specific measurable response.
Push Strategy
A promotional approach that targets channel partners (wholesalers, retailers) to push products toward consumers through trade promotions, slotting fees, and sales training.
Pull Strategy
A promotional approach that promotes directly to consumers, creating demand that "pulls" products through the distribution channel. Uses advertising, social media, and loyalty programs.
Percentage of Sales (Budget)
A budgeting method that allocates a fixed percentage of past or projected sales revenue to promotion. Simple but assumes promotion follows sales rather than driving them.
Competitive Parity (Budget)
A budgeting method that matches competitors' promotional spending levels. Prevents competitive disadvantage but ignores your own unique objectives.
All-You-Can-Afford (Budget)
A budgeting method that allocates whatever funds remain after other expenses. Treats promotion as optional rather than strategic.
Objective & Task (Budget)
The PREFERRED budgeting method. Define objectives → identify required tasks → estimate costs. Strategic and defensible because it starts with goals, not finances.
Flash Sale
A promotional strategy offering significant discounts on select products for a very short period to create urgency and drive immediate purchases.
Personal Selling
A form of promotion in which a salesperson engages a prospective customer directly for the purpose of completing a transaction. The only two-way, real-time promotional mix element.
EKB Model
Engel-Kollat-Blackwell model of consumer decision-making with five stages: Need Recognition → Information Search → Evaluation of Alternatives → Purchase Decision → Post-Purchase Evaluation.
Order-Taking
Sales made through straightforward transactions where little persuasion is needed — the customer has already decided to purchase (e.g., fast food cashier, call center order processor).
Order-Getting
Proactive sales where the salesperson initiates and creates demand by seeking out customers who may not yet recognize a need (e.g., B2B enterprise sales rep, real estate agent).
Prospecting
Step 1 of the selling process — continuously generating leads to fill the sales funnel. A qualified lead meets the MAD criteria: Money, Authority, Desire.
MAD Framework
A lead qualification tool: Money (financial resources to purchase), Authority (decision-making power), Desire (genuine want or need for the product).
Preapproach
Step 2 — gathering information about a prospect before making contact. Includes reviewing CRM data for existing customers and researching new prospects via LinkedIn, industry publications.
Approach
Step 3 — the first real contact with a prospect (in person, video, or phone). The goal is to make a positive first impression and assess readiness to move to the presentation stage.
Presentation
Step 4 — identifying customer needs and demonstrating how the product meets them. Includes handling objections using needs-satisfaction, formula, or stimulus-response approaches.
Needs-Satisfaction Selling
A selling style that personalizes the presentation around the specific customer's needs and usage patterns — most common in consultative B2B sales.
Formula Selling
A selling style that follows a predictable script or pattern. Common objections have rehearsed responses. Effective when products and objections are standard and predictable.
Stimulus–Response Selling
A selling style in which the salesperson adjusts the presentation in real time based on the customer's reactions, body language, and verbal responses. Requires high emotional intelligence.
Close
Step 5 — asking the customer for their business. Without asking, no commitment is secured. Closing finalizes terms, confirms the purchase, processes payment, and arranges delivery.
Follow-Up
Step 6 — confirming that the customer received the product and it meets expectations. Builds trust, addresses service issues, and creates opportunities for repeat business and referrals.
Sales Funnel
The collection of leads being worked by the sales team — from initial prospects down through qualified leads, opportunities, and closed sales.
Sales Management
The strategic leadership of a sales team encompassing planning, recruiting, training, compensation design, leadership/coaching, and results measurement.
Geographic Structure
A sales team organized by region or territory. Pros: local expertise, less travel. Con: reps must sell all products, limiting product depth.
Product/Service Structure
A sales team organized by specific product or service line. Pros: deep technical expertise. Con: same customer may deal with multiple reps.
Customer Structure
Also called account-based structure. Sales reps aligned by customer type or industry vertical. Pros: strong relationship focus. Con: reps need broad product knowledge.
Behavioral Evaluation
Reviewing a salesperson based on HOW they sell (process adherence, relationship quality, ethics) rather than purely on quota attainment or revenue numbers.
Account Management Policies
Rules governing how customers are engaged — covering territory ownership, escalation protocols, contact frequency, pricing authority, and data handling.
