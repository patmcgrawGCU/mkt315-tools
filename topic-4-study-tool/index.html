7 Frameworks

Testable Frameworks at a Glance

Every framework that appears on exams — with the key distinction for each one. Use this as a rapid review before the midterm.

⚡ Exam tip: Scenario questions will describe a situation and ask you to identify which framework element applies. Study the examples in each card — they are the bridge between definition and application.
Product Life Cycle — 4 Stages
Introduction: New to market, build awareness, high promo spend, high risk
Growth: Sales climb, competitors enter, add features & expand distribution
Maturity: Growth slows, steal share, differentiate, adjust pricing
Decline: Sales drop, cut costs, limit distribution, phase out or find niche
⚠ PLC describes a CATEGORY, not a single brand
Brand Development Stages
Awareness: Consumer first notices the brand exists
Recognition: Links brand to a product category
Preference: Repeat purchase begins; favors this brand
Loyalty: Won't switch even at lower competitor price
Brand Equity: Financial value created by loyalty and trust
Packaging — 3 Functions
Protection: Safeguards during transport and storage
Identification: Brand visibility at point of purchase
Messaging: Ingredients, instructions, warnings, portions
Examples: Pringles cylinder, Morton Salt spout, Heinz inverted bottle
4 I's of Services
Intangibility: Cannot be touched before purchase → use reviews, physical cues
Inseparability: Provider IS the service → who you hire defines your service
Inconsistency: Quality varies by provider/day → train and standardize
Inventory: Services can't be stored → manage perishability with pricing
6 Service Quality Dimensions
Reliability #1: Deliver what was promised — the only OUTCOME dimension
Tangibles: Physical setting where service is delivered
Responsiveness: Answer promptly, help quickly
Assurance: Provider has knowledge and competence
Empathy: Understanding the customer's needs and feelings
Tangible Evidence: Customer's evaluation of that physical setting
Gap Model — 5 Gaps
Gap 1 — Knowledge: Don't know what customers want → marketing research
Gap 2 — Standards: Know but didn't build into standards → process design
Gap 3 — Delivery: Standards exist but aren't met → hire, train, supervise
Gap 4 — Communications: Good service, wrong expectations set → consistent messaging
Gap 5 — Customer: Expectation ≠ perception → only fixed by closing Gaps 1–4
CREST Service Recovery
C — Calm the customer first; involve supervisor if needed
R — Repeat the problem to confirm shared understanding
E — Empathy statements: "I understand," "I appreciate..."
S — Solve with a mutually agreed-upon resolution
T — Timely response — delay is a second service failure

🔑 Most Commonly Confused Pairs

Inseparability vs. InconsistencyInseparability = provider IS the service. Inconsistency = quality VARIES across providers and over time. Different concepts, different marketing responses.
Tangibles vs. Tangible EvidenceTangibles = the physical environment (decor, tools). Tangible Evidence = the customer's JUDGMENT of that environment. Same setting, two different concepts.
Product Mix vs. Product LineMix = ALL categories a company offers (width). Line = items within ONE category (depth). Adding a new flavor = line extension. Adding a new category = expanding the mix.
Gap 3 vs. Gap 4Gap 3 = you have the right standards but can't execute them (training problem). Gap 4 = you deliver well but told customers to expect the wrong thing (communication problem).
