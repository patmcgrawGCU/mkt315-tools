lululemon athletica

NASDAQ: LULU  ·  Founded 1998  ·  Vancouver, BC

"Living the sweatlife" — premium athletic apparel rooted in mindfulness and community

Why lululemon for MKT 315? lululemon is a masterclass in premium brand positioning, community-driven marketing, and product-as-lifestyle strategy. The brand has no traditional advertising budget — growth has come almost entirely from grassroots community events, ambassador programs, and word-of-mouth. Your analysis will reveal how a company can build a $50B brand without a single TV commercial.

The palette is intentionally minimal. Red is used sparingly as an accent — black and white carry the vast majority of brand communications, creating a clean, premium feel.

Typography
Wordmark / Brand Name
lululemon athletica
Custom sans-serif, all lowercase. Approachable yet refined. Never capitalize the brand name.
Headlines (Presentations)
Be Present. Be Bold.
Bold sans-serif. Short, active, motivational phrasing. Use Helvetica Neue Bold or Arial Bold as a substitute.
Body / Supporting Text
Designed for people who want to live a life they love.
Clean sans-serif at regular weight. Never use decorative or script fonts.
Brand Voice & Personality

Tone

  • Aspirational but accessible
  • Motivating without being preachy
  • Warm, community-focused
  • Confident, never arrogant

Personality Pillars

  • Mindfulness & intentional living
  • Community over competition
  • Technical performance + style
  • Personal growth & "sweatlife"

Core Positioning

Premium athleisure that bridges performance and lifestyle. The brand sells an identity — belonging to a community of health-conscious, purpose-driven people — not just leggings.

Target Market

Originally "Super Girls" (32-year-old yoga-practicing professionals). Now expanded to men (LULU men's = 23% revenue), Gen Z, and international markets. Core remains affluent, health-conscious consumers ages 22–45.

Brand Positioning
"For health-conscious, active individuals who want performance apparel that supports their entire lifestyle, lululemon is the premium athletic brand that makes technical gear feel like a personal identity — built on community, not advertising." Synthesized from lululemon investor materials & brand guidelines
PowerPoint Presentation Quick Reference
ElementGuideline
Slide backgroundWhite (#FFFFFF) for content; black (#000000) for hero/impact slides. Red (#E20010) only as a rare accent, never as a full background.
Text colorBlack on white. White on black. Never red text — too hard to read at a distance.
Headline fontArial Bold or Helvetica Neue Bold. All caps or sentence case. Never title case in a quirky script.
Imagery styleClean, aspirational, active people in real settings. Bright natural light. Never busy or cluttered. Lots of white space.
Graphic elementsThin lines, minimal geometry. Let the product photography and white space carry the visual weight.
Overall feelPremium, clean, purposeful. Think yoga studio aesthetic, not gym locker room.
Do's & Don'ts
✓ DO
  • Use "lululemon" — always lowercase, even at sentence start
  • Lead with community, lifestyle, and mindfulness themes
  • Use generous white space — never crowd the design
  • Emphasize technical innovation alongside aesthetic appeal
  • Reference the ambassador/community marketing model
✗ DON'T
  • Capitalize to "Lululemon" — this is a brand violation
  • Use red as a background or dominant color
  • Compare directly to Nike/Adidas in a bargain-hunting way
  • Use busy, colorful, cluttered designs
  • Use script fonts or decorative typefaces

Chipotle Mexican Grill

NYSE: CMG  ·  Founded 1993  ·  Denver, CO

"Food With Integrity" — fast-casual dining built on sourcing transparency and customization

Why Chipotle for MKT 315? Chipotle is one of the most studied brands in fast-casual marketing — a rare case where a company disrupted an entire industry category through ethical sourcing, minimal-menu strategy, and digital loyalty programs. Its 2025 campaign directly attacking McDonald's with guerrilla marketing tactics earned 21% same-store sales growth. Rich for every topic in this course.

Warm, earthy tones reinforce "Food With Integrity" — the palette signals real ingredients, not artificial brightness. This intentionally contrasts with McDonald's bright yellow/red.

Typography
Wordmark
CHIPOTLE
Custom modified Gotham Bold with a unique low-midbar "E." Use Gotham Bold or Georgia Bold as presentation substitute.
Headlines
Cultivate a Better World.
Serif headlines reinforce craft and authenticity — differentiating Chipotle from fast-food chains that use sans-serif. Short, values-driven statements.
Body / Subheads
Responsibly sourced. Transparently prepared.
Clean sans-serif at supporting hierarchy level. The contrast of serif headline + sans body is intentional and part of the brand system.
Brand Voice & Personality

Tone

  • Authentic, no-nonsense, transparent
  • Occasionally playful and bold
  • Mission-driven without being sanctimonious
  • Direct: "We charge more because we pay more for better food"

Personality Pillars

  • Food integrity & ethical sourcing
  • Radical customization (the "burrito you build")
  • Transparency about ingredients & process
  • Social & environmental responsibility

Core Positioning

The fast-casual brand that refuses to compromise — on ingredients, sourcing, or principles. "Fast food" without the stigma, premium quality without full-service prices. A third category.

Target Market

Millennials and Gen Z consumers (ages 18–35) who care about what they eat but don't want to pay fine-dining prices. Health-conscious, value-driven, skeptical of corporate "greenwashing."

Brand Positioning
"For conscious consumers who refuse to choose between fast and good, Chipotle is the fast-casual restaurant that proves you don't have to compromise — on ingredients, on ethics, or on flavor." Synthesized from Chipotle investor materials & 'Food With Integrity' platform
PowerPoint Presentation Quick Reference
ElementGuideline
Slide backgroundCream (#F5F0E8) or white for content slides. Chipotle Red (#AC2318) for hero/section dividers. Chipotle Brown (#441500) for dark-mode slides.
Text colorBrown (#441500) on cream/white. White on red or brown. Never red text on a white background — hard to read.
Headline fontGeorgia Bold or a serif font. Reinforce the craft/artisanal positioning with typography choice.
Imagery styleReal food, real people, warm light. Avoid stock-photo perfection — authenticity over polish. Earthy, warm tones in all photography.
Graphic elementsHand-drawn or organic shapes reference the brand's artisanal positioning. Avoid sterile corporate geometry.
Overall feelWarm, honest, grounded. Think a well-designed farmers market, not a tech startup or a fast-food joint.
Do's & Don'ts
✓ DO
  • Lead with "Food With Integrity" as the brand anchor
  • Use warm, earthy tones throughout your presentation
  • Highlight the supply chain ethics and sourcing story
  • Reference the digital loyalty program and data strategy
  • Note the contrast with traditional fast food — this is a category disruptor
✗ DON'T
  • Use bright primary colors (red + yellow) — that's McDonald's
  • Position as a "cheap" option — it's a premium fast-casual brand
  • Use cool blues or corporate grays — wrong brand temperature
  • Ignore the controversies (E. coli outbreaks, price criticism) — they're real case study material
  • Use sans-serif headlines — serif is intentional and brand-correct

Duolingo

NASDAQ: DUOL  ·  Founded 2011  ·  Pittsburgh, PA

"The world's best way to learn a language" — gamified education with a memorably chaotic brand personality

Why Duolingo for MKT 315? Duolingo is one of the most distinctive digital marketing case studies of the 2020s. The brand built a massive social media following by giving its mascot (Duo the owl) a darkly humorous, meme-driven personality that no other edtech company would dare attempt. It is also a textbook example of freemium pricing, data-driven product design, and conversion funnel optimization through gamification.

Each color in the product palette has a purpose: green = progress, yellow = streaks, red = lives/hearts, blue = trust and learning, purple = premium/Super. Use this systematically — colors communicate meaning.

Typography
Primary Brand Font
DIN Round Pro
Rounded, friendly, bold. Use Impact or Arial Black as presentation substitute. The rounded letterforms signal approachability and playfulness.
Headlines & CTAs
Learn for free. Forever.
Ultra-bold, short, punchy. Duolingo headlines are never more than 5–6 words. Always direct. Often slightly cheeky.
Body & UI Text
You're on a 7-day streak. Don't blow it.
Bold body text with a conversational, slightly taunting tone. The brand speaks directly to users — no corporate distance.
Brand Voice & Personality

Tone

  • Playful, witty, occasionally threatening
  • Self-aware and meme-literate
  • Encouraging but with mock urgency ("Don't break your streak")
  • Personality-forward: Duo the owl IS the brand

Personality Pillars

  • Gamification of learning (streaks, XP, leagues)
  • Accessibility — free forever for core product
  • Data-driven personalization
  • Humor as a marketing differentiator

Core Positioning

The only language-learning app that makes you actually come back every day — through a combination of gamification, social accountability, and a mascot that guilt-trips you in ways you weirdly enjoy.

Target Market

Gen Z and Millennials (16–35) globally. Casual learners and language hobbyists, not serious academics. People who've "always wanted to learn Spanish" but need motivation, not a classroom.

Brand Positioning
"For people who want to learn a language but struggle with consistency, Duolingo is the free, gamified app that turns daily practice into a habit — through progress systems, social competition, and a green owl that will not leave you alone." Synthesized from Duolingo brand & product strategy
PowerPoint Presentation Quick Reference
ElementGuideline
Slide backgroundWhite for content. Duo Green (#58CC02) for hero/opener. Off-black for dark-mode data slides. Use the product color system (blue, yellow, red) to organize content sections.
Text colorDark (#1A1A1A) on white or green. White on dark. Always high contrast — the brand is designed for mobile readability.
Headline fontImpact, Arial Black, or any ultra-bold rounded sans-serif. Bold, chunky, legible at a glance.
Imagery styleBright, saturated, flat illustration style. The product UI screenshots. Duo the owl (reference only — don't reproduce the trademarked character). Avoid moody or subtle photography.
Graphic elementsRounded rectangles, bold icons, progress bars. The gamification UI aesthetic works as a design system for your slides.
Overall feelFun, energetic, slightly absurd. This is not a conservative brand — your presentation should have personality.
Do's & Don'ts
✓ DO
  • Use vibrant, bold colors throughout — this is a high-energy brand
  • Lean into the freemium model as a core business strategy discussion
  • Reference the social media / TikTok brand strategy as a marketing case study
  • Use the gamification elements (streaks, hearts, leaderboards) in your IMC analysis
  • Discuss the brand's data analytics depth — it tracks everything
✗ DON'T
  • Use dull, corporate, or muted colors — totally wrong brand register
  • Position Duolingo as an academic/educational "serious" brand
  • Use thin or light-weight fonts — bold is the entire brand's typographic identity
  • Reproduce Duo the owl as if it were a simple clip-art element — he's a complex IP asset
  • Ignore the tension between free product and paid Super Duolingo conversion

Dutch Bros Coffee

NYSE: BROS  ·  Founded 1992  ·  Grants Pass, OR

"Speed of Service, Quality of Life" — community-first drive-thru coffee culture from the Pacific Northwest

Why Dutch Bros for MKT 315? Dutch Bros is the anti-Starbucks story — a brand built not on premium positioning but on radical human connection. With almost no traditional advertising, it grew from a single pushcart to 900+ locations through community events, employee culture, and a loyalty app. The founders' openly faith-driven values make it a rare case for the Christian worldview element in your Topic 8 presentation.

The tricolor stripe (yellow, orange/red, blue) references Dutch tulip fields and the Netherlands flag — connecting the brand to the Boersma brothers' heritage. Blue is the dominant brand color for trust and reliability.

Typography
Brand Wordmark
DUTCH BROS
Bold condensed uppercase block letters in blue. The brand name is always in caps. "Coffee" appears in a looser, warmer treatment beneath — contrast between bold corporate and friendly script.
Headlines
MAKE IT COUNT.
Bold, uppercase, action-oriented. Dutch Bros headlines are energetic and community-focused. Use Arial Black or Impact as substitutes.
Supporting Voice
Good people making great drinks — and making your day a little better.
Warm, conversational body copy. The brand speaks like a friend, not a corporation. Never formal or stiff.
Brand Voice & Personality

Tone

  • High-energy, positive, genuinely warm
  • Community-focused — "broista" culture is the brand
  • Fun without being performative
  • Faith-influenced: service, generosity, caring for others

Personality Pillars

  • Speed + human connection (not a contradiction)
  • Community giving & fundraiser days
  • Highly customized drinks ("secret menu")
  • Employee culture as brand identity

Core Positioning

The coffee chain where the experience is the product. You can get a good drink anywhere — Dutch Bros sells a connection. Every interaction is designed to make the customer's day better, not just faster.

Target Market

Gen Z and younger Millennials (16–30), primarily in Western U.S. markets. Value-seekers who still want variety and customization. Brand loyalists drawn to the community energy and employee-customer relationship.

Brand Positioning
"For coffee lovers who want more than a transaction, Dutch Bros is the drive-thru brand that treats every customer like a regular — built on genuine human connection, community generosity, and drinks you can make uniquely yours." Synthesized from Dutch Bros investor materials & brand mission

Do's & Don'ts
✓ DO
  • Lead with community and human connection — this is the brand's core differentiator
  • Use the Dutch heritage (windmill, tulip colors) as a visual anchor
  • Note the faith-founded values when addressing the Christian worldview element
  • Discuss the loyalty app and data strategy for the analytics topic
  • Compare and contrast with Starbucks — same category, totally different brand strategy
✗ DON'T
  • Use a quiet, minimal, coffee-shop aesthetic — that's Starbucks territory
  • Position Dutch Bros as a premium luxury brand — it's accessible and value-positive

  • Use dark, moody photography — the brand lives in bright, warm, energetic spaces
  • Ignore the rapid national expansion story — it's central to the distribution analysis

Coach  / Tapestry, Inc.

NYSE: TPR  ·  Coach est. 1941  ·  New York, NY

"New York style. Global reach." — accessible luxury accessories brand undergoing a Gen Z transformation

Why Coach / Tapestry for MKT 315? Coach is a brand management case study unlike any other on this list. As one of three brands under the Tapestry umbrella (alongside Kate Spade and Stuart Weitzman), it gives you the rare opportunity to analyze how a parent company manages multiple distinct brand identities simultaneously. Coach's "Amplify Plan" to win Gen Z shoppers, its Coachtopia sustainability sub-brand, and its comeback from a decade of brand dilution make it rich material for every topic in the course.

Coach's palette is rooted in leather craft heritage — saddle tans and natural hides communicate the brand's origin as a Manhattan leather workshop. Black grounds everything in New York sophistication. Recent campaigns introduce seasonal accent colors, but the core never strays from this foundation.

Understanding the Tapestry Brand Portfolio

Coach

The flagship brand. Accessible luxury handbags, accessories, and ready-to-wear. Gen Z pivot underway. 900+ stores globally. The "big sister" brand.

Kate Spade New York

Colorful, optimistic, playful luxury. Targets women 25–40. Distinctive spade logo. More affordable price point than Coach. Strong gift and occasion purchasing.

Stuart Weitzman

Luxury footwear. Smallest of the three brands. Celebrity-worn. Targets affluent women 30–55. Known for the stretch-fit boot innovation.

Why This Matters for MKT 315

Tapestry demonstrates brand portfolio management — how one parent company maintains three distinct identities without cannibalizing each other. This is core brand management curriculum.

Typography
Brand Wordmark
COACH
Wide-tracked uppercase serif. The stencil-quality letterforms reference the brand's heritage as a luggage and travel goods maker. Regal and timeless.
Headlines
Crafted in New York since 1941.
Elegant serif, often italic for editorial use. Headlines can be long and poetic — this brand has a luxury pace, not a fast-food urgency.
Modern Campaign Voice (Gen Z Pivot)
The Lil Nas X Bag. Your Rules.
For Gen Z campaigns, Coach introduces bolder sans-serif language and celebrity/artist collaborations. The brand is intentionally bridging its heritage voice with a younger register.
Brand Voice & Personality

Tone

  • Confident, crafted, New York cool
  • Heritage-proud but culturally current
  • Warm and inclusive — "accessible luxury"
  • Recently: collaborations with artists and Gen Z icons

Personality Pillars

  • Craftsmanship — every product has a heritage story
  • American luxury (not European pretension)
  • Inclusivity and self-expression ("Coachtopia")
  • Cultural relevance through artist collaborations

Core Positioning

The accessible luxury brand that lets you own a piece of New York craftsmanship — refined enough for boardrooms, contemporary enough for culture, and priced between fast fashion and true luxury.

Target Market

Historically: women 25–45, aspirational middle-to-upper class. Current pivot: Gen Z (18–25) as new customer acquisition priority. Per Tapestry's 2025 report, 2/3 of new North American customers are Gen Z.

Brand Positioning
"For individuals who want to express their identity through quality craftsmanship, Coach is the New York-born accessible luxury brand that bridges heritage and culture — offering beautifully made accessories that feel earned, not exclusive." Synthesized from Coach / Tapestry investor materials & 2025 Amplify Plan

Do's & Don'ts
✓ DO
  • Distinguish between Coach (brand) and Tapestry, Inc. (parent company) clearly
  • Analyze the Gen Z pivot as a brand repositioning case study
  • Discuss the brand portfolio management challenge — three brands, one parent
  • Use the failed Capri/Versace merger attempt as a strategic decision-making example
  • Reference the Coachtopia circular sub-brand for the ethics/sustainability discussion
✗ DON'T
  • Confuse Coach with ultra-luxury (Louis Vuitton, Hermès) — it is accessible luxury
  • Use bright, fun colors — Coach is sophisticated and restrained in its palette
  • Ignore the brand dilution period (2010s discount strategy) — it's critical context
  • Use generic stock photos — imagery quality signals brand quality in presentations
  • Overlook the Kate Spade and Stuart Weitzman brands when discussing brand portfolio strategy
